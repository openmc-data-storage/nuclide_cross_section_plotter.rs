Privacy

Nuclide Cross Section Plotter

Short version

This site sets no cookies, stores nothing in your browser, runs no analytics and has no accounts. Every file the page loads comes from this site itself, so opening it contacts no advertiser, tracker or content network.

Who is responsible

Jon Shimwell is the controller for this site. For anything on this page, including a request to exercise the rights below, write to mail@jshimwell.com.

What the page stores on your device

Nothing. There are no cookies, no local storage and no session storage. The plot you build is held in the address bar, after the #, which is why a plot can be shared as a link. That text never leaves your browser unless you copy the link and send it yourself.

What happens when you load the page

The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, so GitHub Inc. receives your IP address and records it in server logs in order to deliver the page and to protect the service from abuse. Cross section data is fetched on demand from yamc-data.xsplot.com, which is served through Cloudflare, Inc., so Cloudflare receives your IP address for the same purposes when you tick a reaction. Both are United States companies operating from their EU points of presence, and both act under their standard data processing terms. Neither the controller nor this site keeps any record of who visited or what they plotted.

Legal basis and retention

The only processing is the delivery and security logging described above, which relies on legitimate interests under Article 6(1)(f) GDPR: a website cannot be served without receiving the request. Retention of those logs is set by GitHub and Cloudflare under their own policies. No separate records are created or kept here.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you may request access to personal data concerning you, its correction or erasure, a restriction of processing, and you may object to processing based on legitimate interests. Since no visitor records are kept here, in practice there is nothing to hand over or delete, but write to the address above and it will be answered. You may also complain to a data protection supervisory authority in the country where you live or work.
